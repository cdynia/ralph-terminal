Insider-following, written down before it's tested

A strategy only counts if its rules exist before the first trade. This is Ralph's: where the signals come from, exactly when it buys and sells, and the three gates it must pass before a single real dollar is at risk.

Status: draft. Not yet backtested, not yet paper-traded. The performance numbers below are from the academic record, not from Ralph. Until the gates are passed, every claim here is a hypothesis.

The thesis

Executives buying their own stock with their own money — especially several of them in the same two weeks — is the best-documented public signal in equities (~82 bps/month abnormal return for opportunistic buys). Congressional purchase disclosures have beaten the index in live products. Both are public record. Ralph trades the paper trail, holds for weeks, and lets the risk engine veto anything oversized.

signal reversalthe same insiders start selling → exit immediately

The three gates before real money

These run in order, and each one can kill the strategy. That's the point.

  1. Backtest. Positive expectancy on 3+ years of historical filings, including a down market. Fail → back to the drawing board, no trades.
  2. Paper record. At least 20 signals or 8 weeks on the simulated account: expectancy positive, max drawdown under 10%. Fail → revise or retire.
  3. Live at minimum size. $250-per-order caps, both owners sign off. Size grows only as the live scorecard earns it.

What could go wrong (the honest list)

  • Congressional disclosures lag up to 45 days — Ralph buys late, and the edge may shrink as copy-trading products crowd in.
  • The insider edge concentrates in smaller companies: wider spreads, gappier stops, worse fills than the studies assume.
  • Recent out-of-sample tests of naive cluster screens show a weakened edge — the strict filters above are the whole game, and they might not be enough.
  • This is a weeks-to-months strategy. It will be boring. Boring is the point.
